Math What is a ratio?
A ratio compares how many of one thing you have compared to everything else. Think of sorting your candy bag — just counting parts.
Math The Marble Example 🔴🔵
3 red + 2 blue = 5 total.
What fraction are red? → Put what you want on top, total on bottom: 3/5
Like pizza slices — 3 of 5 have pepperoni, so 3/5 of the pizza is pepperoni!
Math Solving a Proportion
Ratio of boys to girls = 2:3. There are 12 boys. How many girls?
2/3 = 12/girls → cross-multiply → girls = 18
  • Part / Whole → fraction of the whole group
  • Cross multiply to solve a proportion
  • Don't forget to add the parts to get the total first!

Math Logs look scary. They're not.
A log asks one question: "What power do I raise this number to?"

You know 2³ = 8. A log asks the same thing backwards: "What power of 2 gives me 8?"
2³ = 8  ↔  log₂(8) = 3
Math Try it: log₂(32)
Ask: 2 to what power = 32? Just count up!
2¹=2 · 2²=4 · 2³=8 · 2⁴=16 · 2⁵=32 ← stop!
log₂(32) = 5 ✅
Logs are like stairs — count how many steps to reach the top floor.
Math 3-Step Log Method
  • Step 1: Find the base (little subscript number)
  • Step 2: Count up: base¹, base²… until you hit the big number
  • Step 3: That count = your answer
  • Don't panic — "log" just means what power?

English The Golden Rule: Shorter = Better
On ACT English, the shortest correct answer is almost always right. If two answers mean the same thing, always pick fewer words.
Like texting — you'd say "meet at the mall," not "proceed to the location of the mall."
English Stacked Synonyms — Cut One
When two words mean the same thing side-by-side, one has to go.
"today's modern society"
↓ Both words mean "right now" — delete one!

"today's society" ✅
English Bloated Phrases → One Word
"was of the opinion that" → "believed"
"in the event that" → "if"
"due to the fact that" → "because"
  • Cut it if the sentence still makes sense without it
  • "OMIT the underlined portion" is often the right answer
  • Never pick longer just because it sounds fancier

English The Golden Rule of Semicolons
Both sides of a semicolon MUST be a complete sentence (subject + verb, makes sense alone).
"I studied hard; I aced the test." ✅
Both sides = complete sentences ✅

"I studied hard; because I wanted an A."
❌ "because I wanted an A" is NOT a complete sentence
English The "Then" Trap 🚦
"Then" is an adverb — NOT a conjunction. You can't join two sentences with just comma + "then."
"I ate dinner, then I watched TV." ❌

"I ate dinner; then I watched TV." ✅
"I ate dinner, and then I watched TV." ✅
A semicolon is a seesaw — both sides must be equal (complete sentences) or it tips over! ⚖️
English How to Test a Semicolon on the ACT
  • Read LEFT side. Is it a complete sentence? ✅
  • Read RIGHT side. Is it a complete sentence? ✅
  • Both pass → semicolon is correct!
  • Either side fails → semicolon is wrong. Use a comma or rewrite.

Math Slope = How Steep is the Hill? 🏔️
Slope tells you how steep a line is. Steep hill = big slope. Flat path = zero slope.
slope (m) = (y₂ – y₁) / (x₂ – x₁)
A staircase: Rise = how tall each step is. Run = how deep. Slope = rise/run.
Math Example: Points (1, 2) and (4, 8)
slope = (8 – 2) / (4 – 1) = 6 / 3 = 2 ✅

For every 1 step right → 2 steps up. That's a slope of 2!
Math Reading y = mx + b Instantly
y = mx + b
m = slope (number in front of x)
b = y-intercept (where it crosses the y-axis)

y = 3x + 5 → slope = 3, y-intercept = 5
  • Positive slope ↗ uphill | Negative slope ↘ downhill
  • Don't flip it! Rise on TOP, run on bottom. Always.
